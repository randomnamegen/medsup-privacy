# Privacy Policy — MedSup

**Last updated: October 5, 2026** — renamed *Sharing a list with your doctor* to

to send it to. And the *Photos* permission now says which phones ask for it. Nothing
about what MedSup collects changed; it is still nothing.

MedSup is a medication supply tracker. This policy explains what it does
with your information. The short version: it does not collect any of it.

## We do not collect your information

MedSup has no account, no server, and no analytics. It does not send your
information anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to send it to.

Specifically, the app does **not**:

- create an account or ask you to sign in
- upload or transmit your information anywhere
- use analytics, crash reporting, advertising, or tracking of any kind
- share or sell anything to anyone
- contain any third-party service that receives your data

Nobody can see what you enter — including us — unless you share a list of it
yourself, and then only whoever you send it to. We have no way to access it.

## What is stored, and where

Everything you enter stays on your own device, in the app's private storage:

- **Medications** — name, strength, dosage, how often you take them, and how
  many you have
- **Photos** — any picture you take or choose to help you recognize a medication
- **Your own notes** — anything you write in a medication's directions or
  notes field
- **Pharmacy details** — the name, phone number and website you enter
- **Doctor's details** — the name and phone number you enter under *Doctor*,
  for whoever prescribed it. These are somebody else's contact details, and the
  app also remembers them to fill in the next medication you add
- **Dose history** — which doses you marked as taken or skipped, and any dose
  you logged as needed, kept for about 13 months and then discarded, along with
  the streak counted from it
- **Your settings** — your first name if you give one, appearance, reminders,
  and how you prefer to track

This information is readable only by MedSup on your device. It is not
placed in shared storage, and other apps cannot read it.

## Sharing your medication list

**Settings → Share your medication list** makes a PDF of your medications and
opens your phone's share sheet. You choose where it goes — an email, a message,
a printer, a file — and it goes only there. Nothing is sent to us, and we never
see it.

The list holds each medication you take now, with its strength, dose and times,
its directions and notes, and its doctor and pharmacy; the medications you have
stopped, and when; and how many doses you marked as taken in the last 30 days.
It does not include your photos or your name.

Three things worth knowing about it:

- **Once you send it, that copy is wherever you sent it**, under that app's
  terms rather than ours. Deleting it there is up to you.
- **It is not encrypted, even with a PIN set.** It is made for someone else to
  read.
- **MedSup keeps the last list it made** in its private storage, which other
  apps cannot read, until you make the next one, which replaces it, or clear all
  data. Your phone's backup never includes it.

## Permissions, and why they exist

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| **Notifications** | To remind you to take a dose or refill. Reminders are created and scheduled entirely on your device; nothing is sent through any server. |
| **Camera** | Only if you choose to photograph a medication. The photo is saved on your device. |
| **Photos** | Only on Android 12 and older, and only if you choose an existing picture instead of taking one. Newer phones open Android's own photo picker instead, which needs no permission and gives MedSup only the picture you choose. |
| **Vibration** | For the short buzz when you tap a button, and for reminders. Can be turned off in Settings. |
| **Alarms & reminders** | So a reminder arrives at the minute you set rather than whenever the system next decides to wake the app. Android asks for this one separately, on its own settings screen. Without it MedSup schedules **no** reminders at all and tells you why — a reminder that arrives an hour late is worse than none, because a missing one gets noticed and a late one just looks like the time you chose. |

Every one of these is optional. Declining any of them leaves the rest of the
app working.

## Your phone's own backup

MedSup does not back anything up. Your phone might, and you should know
that before you decide what to put in it.

Android can include an app's data in the device backup you have already set
up, through Google Drive. If that is switched on, MedSup's information is
part of it. That copy sits in **your** Google account, under your own
credentials, covered by Google's terms. We cannot see it, cannot ask for it,
and have no way to reach it.

You control this in your phone's backup settings, not in this app. Turning the
device backup off, or excluding MedSup from it, stops the copy being made.

**Two things MedSup decides about that copy**, rather than leaving them
to the default:

- **Your photos are excluded from it.** They stay on the phone they were taken
  on. The trade is that a restore onto a new phone brings your medications and
  not their pictures, and that is deliberate: a photograph of a pill can be
  looked up as easily as a name can, so it should not be the part that travels
  in the clear.
- **If you have set a PIN, what does travel is already encrypted**, and your
  recovery code is the only thing that opens it on the new phone. That is the
  whole reason the recovery code exists and the reason it is worth writing
  down.

## Deleting your information

Deleting a medication removes it and its photo from your device. **Settings →
Clear all data** removes everything at once — every medication, every dose you
have logged, every photo, every reminder, every setting, and the last list you
shared — and returns the app to its first run. A list you have already sent is
wherever you sent it, and is not something MedSup can reach. On the rare occasion
the phone refuses to delete a photo file straight away, that file is queued and
removed the next time you open the app, rather than being left behind.
Uninstalling MedSup also removes everything the app stored there.

If your phone's backup includes MedSup, a copy can outlive the uninstall
and be restored later to a new device. Removing that copy is done through your
phone's backup settings, for the same reason as above: it is in your account,
not ours.

There is no data for you to request, export, or have erased by us, because we
never receive any.

## What this does not protect against

Everything above is about keeping your information away from us and off the
network. That is not the same as keeping it away from someone holding your
phone, and it would be dishonest to let the two blur together:

- **Without a PIN set, anyone who can unlock your device can open MedSup
  and read all of it** — your medications and notes, photos, pharmacy and
  doctor's details, and dose history.
- **Reminders can show a medication's name** on your lock screen or in your
  notification shade, where someone glancing at your phone could read it. This
  is true whether or not you set a PIN: reminders are handed to the operating
  system ahead of time so they can arrive when the app is closed, and they sit
  in its notification database with their text in them. Setting **what
  reminders show** to *Hide names* is the only thing that changes this, and it
  is what to use if that matters to you.
- Your information is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot
  read, and your phone encrypts that storage as it does all app data.

## Setting a PIN encrypts what is stored

If you set a PIN under **Settings → App lock**, MedSup encrypts your
medications, your dose history — both the doses you take on a schedule and the
ones you log as needed — your streak, the pharmacy and doctor the form
offers to reuse, and its list of leftover photo files, before writing them
down. The key is protected by your PIN and by your recovery code, and it exists
only while the app is open — so those records cannot be read off the device
without one of the two, by us or by anyone else.

Being exact about the limits, because a half-true claim here is worse than
none:

- **It only applies while a PIN is set.** Without one there is no key, and
  everything is stored as it always was. Turning the PIN off decrypts
  everything back again, on purpose: it is the only way the app can still open
  your records once the key is gone.
- **Your photos are not encrypted**, and that is a decision rather than
  something waiting to be done. They stay as ordinary files in the app's
  private storage, which other apps cannot read. They are saved under names
  like `pill-mf3k2a-x7q1.jpg`, with no medication name in them, and the record
  saying which photo belongs to which medication is inside what the PIN
  encrypts. So a photo on its own does not say what it is or whose it is. A
  picture of a labeled box still shows whatever is printed on the box, which
  is worth knowing before you take one.
- **Your name and the end-of-day check-in time are not encrypted.** The app
  needs them before it can ask for your PIN, to greet you and to draw the lock
  screen correctly.
- **Your settings are not encrypted** — appearance, haptics, how reminders
  arrive and what they show. They are not health information.
- **A list you share is not encrypted**, and neither is the copy MedSup keeps
  until you make the next one. See *Sharing your medication list*.
- **If you forget both your PIN and your recovery code, the encrypted records
  cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.** That is what the encryption
  means. The app will offer to start you over from empty, which is the only
  thing it can do.

## App stores

You will have obtained MedSup from Google Play. Google collects its own
information about downloads and devices under its own policy, which we do not
control and have no access to. This policy covers only the app itself.

## Children

MedSup is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly
collect information from anyone — of any age.

## Medical disclaimer

MedSup is a tool for keeping track of medication supply. It is not medical
advice. It does not know your prescription, cannot check whether medications
interact, and cannot tell whether you have actually taken a dose. Always follow
the label on your medication and the guidance of your pharmacist or doctor.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change with it. Because the app
collects nothing, we do not expect substantive changes unless the app itself
gains a feature that handles information differently — in which case this page
will say so before that feature ships.
